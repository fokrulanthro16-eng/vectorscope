Gainesville VectorScope Architecture

NASA EMERGE & UF GeoDI Lab Research Alignment

1. Research & Mission Context

Developed for the CityCamp Gainesville Hack Day (NASA EMERGE Track), Gainesville VectorScope operationalizes remote sensing methodologies published by the University of Florida (UF) GeoDI Lab and Florida Community Innovation. The platform bridges satellite observations with municipal public works and vector control field operations.

2. Earth Observations Integration

  • MODIS Land Surface Temperature (LST): Ingested to drive non-linear thermal kinetics under the calibrated Briére-1 performance equation.
  • NASA GPM (Global Precipitation Measurement): 72-hour cumulative rainfall informs hydrological surface retention and stagnant pooling risk.
  • Landsat-9 Operational Land Imager: Multi-spectral Normalized Difference Water Index (NDWI) and Vegetative Index (NDVI) assess surface saturation and tree canopy thermal buffering.

3. The Briére-1 Thermal Incubation Engine

Mosquito embryonic and larval development do not respond linearly to temperature. We apply the Briére-1 equation:

Rate(T) = c · T · (T - T_min) · √(T_max - T)   [T_min = 16°C, T_max = 38°C, T_opt ≈ 29°C]

Between 27°C and 30°C, pupation velocity peaks, compressing egg-to-adult emergence times by up to 50%.

4. Environmental Justice & Community Equity

Historical urban development disparities in East Gainesville result in lower canopy shading and unpaved, aging ditch stormwater infrastructure. VectorScope explicitly flags these neighborhoods as Priority 1 Environmental Justice Zones to guide municipal resource allocation before chemical adulticides are mandated.

5. AI Vector Copilot (Gemini API)

The integrated Google Gemini copilot translates real-time hydrological scores into actionable field protocols (e.g., Bacillus thuringiensis israelensis granules, Gambusia holbrooki biological stocking, or civic container clearance sweeps). An autonomous offline fallback ensures unbroken execution even without API connectivity.

1. Briére-1 Non-Linear Thermal Performance Curve

Briére et al. (1999) / UF GeoDI Calibrated

Mosquito embryonic, larval, and pupal development velocities respond non-linearly to environmental temperatures. Below $T_{min}$ ($16^\circ\text{C}$), metabolic enzymes cease function; above $T_{max}$ ($38^\circ\text{C}$), lethal protein denaturation occurs. The rate function peaks sharply at $T_{opt} \approx 29^\circ\text{C}$:

Rate(T) = c · T · (T - T_min) · √(max(0, T_max - T))

Empirical Validation: Calibrated for Aedes albopictus and Culex quinquefasciatus in North Central Florida. A post-storm temperature jump from 23°C to 29°C compresses egg-to-adult emergence from 12 days to only 6.2 days.

2. NASA Sensor Band Mathematical Formulations

Landsat-9 OLI-2 & MODIS Terra
Landsat-9 OLI-2 Normalized Difference Vegetation Index (NDVI)
NDVI = (Band 5_NIR - Band 4_Red) / (Band 5_NIR + Band 4_Red)

Calculates sub-canopy shading index (0.85-0.88µm vs 0.64-0.67µm).

Landsat-9 OLI-2 Normalized Difference Water Index (NDWI)
NDWI = (Band 3_Green - Band 5_NIR) / (Band 3_Green + Band 5_NIR)

Surfaces open water retention, but blind to sub-canopy ditch pools.

MODIS Split-Window Land Surface Temperature (LST)
LST = T31 + c1(T31 - T32) + c2(T31 - T32)^2 + (a + b·W)(1 - ε)

Split-window 11.0µm (B31) & 12.0µm (B32) with atmospheric water vapor.
